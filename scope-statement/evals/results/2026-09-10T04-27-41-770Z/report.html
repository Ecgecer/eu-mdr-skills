criteriallm

PASS requires ALL of: (1) A block with the labelled lines, including a "Not checked" line and a "does not establish" line (wording may vary; the two functions must both be present). # Compliance Module — Scope Statement

**Status:** Draft · **Date:** 2026-09-10

## 1. Purpose

The compliance module evaluates a customer submission against the regulatory requirements in force at the time of evaluation and returns a determination of **pass** or **fail**.

It is a decision component, not a workflow system. It answers one question — "does this submission meet the requirements?" — and reports the answer with enough detail for a human to understand and defend it.

## 2. In Scope

- **Submission intake.** Accept a structured submission from a calling service; reject malformed or incomplete input before evaluation, with a distinct error signal (not a `fail` — see §5).
- **Rule evaluation.** Apply the active ruleset to the submission, producing a per-rule outcome.
- **Determination.** Aggregate per-rule outcomes into a single `pass`/`fail`. Any failed mandatory rule fails the submission.
- **Reason reporting.** Return identifiers for *every* failed rule, not just the first — callers need the complete set to give customers actionable remediation.
- **Ruleset versioning.** Every determination records the ruleset version applied and the evaluation timestamp.
- **Audit record.** Emit an immutable record per evaluation: submission id, ruleset version, per-rule outcomes, determination, timestamp.
- **Ruleset loading.** Load the active ruleset from the designated source of truth at startup and on update, validating the ruleset is well-formed before it goes active.

## 3. Out of Scope

- Authoring or legally interpreting the requirements. The module consumes a ruleset; it does not decide what the rules say.
- Collecting submissions from customers (UI, forms, upload).
- Notifying customers of results, or any remediation workflow.
- Manual review, override, appeal, or exception handling. A `fail` is an *input* to those processes, which live elsewhere.
- Storing submissions beyond what the audit record requires.
- Retroactive re-evaluation of past submissions when rules change (see §6).
- Partial or scored determinations — the output is binary.

## 4. Inputs and Outputs

**Input:** a structured submission with a stable identifier and the fields the active ruleset requires.

**Output:**

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `determination` | `pass` or `fail` |
| `ruleset_version` | Version of the ruleset applied |
| `evaluated_at` | Evaluation timestamp |
| `failed_rules` | Identifiers of all unsatisfied rules (empty on pass) |
| `submission_id` | Echoed submission identifier |

## 5. Boundary Conditions

Three outcomes are distinct and must not be collapsed:

1. **Pass** — evaluated, all mandatory rules satisfied.
2. **Fail** — evaluated, one or more mandatory rules not satisfied.
3. **Not evaluable** — could not be evaluated (malformed input, missing required field, no active ruleset). This is an error, not a `fail`.

Conflating "not evaluable" with "fail" would tell a customer they violated a requirement when the system in fact could not assess them.

## 6. Assumptions

Assumptions, not established facts — confirm before build.

- **A single regulatory regime applies.** "Current regulatory requirements" is read as one authoritative ruleset, not a per-jurisdiction matrix. If regime varies by customer location, product, or entity type, ruleset *selection* becomes part of scope and this statement needs revision.
- **A machine-readable source of truth for the ruleset exists or will be provided.** The module does not scrape or interpret regulatory text.
- **"Current" means current at evaluation time.** A determination is a statement about one submission under one ruleset version at one moment; it does not stay true when rules change. Nothing here re-evaluates past submissions — if the business needs that, it is separate scope.
- **Rules are evaluable from the submission alone**, without external third-party lookups.

## 7. Acceptance Criteria

- A submission meeting all mandatory rules returns `pass`.
- A violating submission returns `fail` and lists every violated rule.
- A malformed submission returns not-evaluable, never `fail`.
- Every determination carries ruleset version and timestamp.
- Same submission + same ruleset version ⇒ deterministic, repeatable result.
- An audit record exists for every evaluation, including fails and not-evaluables.

## 8. Open Questions

1. Which regime(s), and can one submission be subject to more than one?
2. What is the source of truth for the ruleset, and who owns updates?
3. Are all rules mandatory, or are some advisory (warn without failing)?
4. Required retention period for audit records?
5. Expected volume and latency budget — determines synchronous vs. queued evaluation.
