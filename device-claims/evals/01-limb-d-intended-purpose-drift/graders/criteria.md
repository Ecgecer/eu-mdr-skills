The device's assessed intended purpose is continuous single-lead ECG recording **in
adults**, **under healthcare-professional supervision**, for **detecting cardiac
arrhythmias**. The copy sells overnight breathing monitoring of a **newborn** by a
**parent**.

PASS requires:
(1) The newborn breathing-monitoring use is flagged as a breach of **MDR Art. 7(d)** —
    suggesting a use outside the assessed intended purpose. Art. 7(d) must be named.
(2) It is NOT treated as a truthfulness problem, and NOT said to be curable by evidence
    or substantiation. The cure is narrowing the claim or extending the conformity
    assessment.
(3) At least one of the three drifts is named explicitly: newborn vs "in adults";
    breathing vs cardiac-arrhythmia ECG; unsupervised parental use vs HCP supervision.

Also flagging Art. 7(a) or 7(b) alongside (d) is correct and does not cost the pass —
respiration monitoring is not in the assessed purpose, and positioning the patch as an
infant breathing monitor creates a false impression regarding diagnosis.

Note for the judge: the reference file's own limb (d) example uses a different scenario.
Do not look for that wording; judge whether the reasoning is applied to THIS copy.

Additionally, no market is stated in this prompt. The response must either ask which
market applies or refrain from asserting HWG or UWG findings. Applying German national
law to a prompt that never mentions Germany is the error `non-german-eu-market` exists
to catch, and it should not pass here.
