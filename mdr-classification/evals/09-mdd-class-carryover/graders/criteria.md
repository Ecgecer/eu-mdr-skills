Jurisdiction bleed: an MDD-era class carried into an MDR question. Under the MDD,
standalone software was classified through the active-device rules and often landed in
Class I. MDR Annex VIII has its own software rule, and classification starts again on
the MDR rules regardless of the MDD result.

Intended purpose: indicating possible atrial fibrillation to GPs is information used
to take decisions with diagnostic purposes, so Rule 11 limb 1 applies: at least IIa,
with escalation to be considered by the impact of a wrong decision.

PASS requires:
(1) It rejects carrying the MDD class over and classifies under MDR Annex VIII, and
(2) it identifies Rule 11 limb 1, so at least IIa, and does not supply a Class I rule
    number.

Mentioning that a device classified I under the MDD that now needs a notified body may
have had a transition route under Art. 120(3c), subject to its conditions, PASSES,
provided the classification itself is MDR-based. Raising 3.3 or 3.5 is good but not
required.

FAIL if it confirms Class I, cites an MDD rule as applying under MDR, or names an MDR
rule as the basis for Class I on this intended purpose.
