## The distinction, as MDCG 2019-11 draws it

The operative provision is **Decision Step 3** of the qualification decision tree, roughly:

> Software which does not perform an action on data, or performs an action limited to storage, archival, communication, "simple search" or lossless compression, is not a medical device software.

And the accompanying footnote defining the term:

> "Simple search" refers to the retrieval of records by matching record metadata against record search criteria or to the retrieval of information. Simple search does not include software which provides interpretative search results (e.g. to identify medical findings in health records).

That footnote *is* the official position on your question. There is no separate "adds value" doctrine — the line MDCG draws is **interpretative vs. non-interpretative**, not valuable vs. not valuable.

Supporting chain:
- **MDR Article 2(1)** — the definition of "medical device"; intended purpose is what qualifies software, not its mechanics.
- **MDR Recital 19** — software in its own right is a device when intended for a medical purpose; software for general purposes, even used in healthcare, is not.
- **MDR Annex VIII, Rule 11** — the classification rule if it *does* qualify. Note the consequence: Rule 11 pushes decision-support software to **Class IIa minimum**, IIb/III where death or serious deterioration is in play. So the qualification question isn't academic; failing it means a notified body.
- **MEDDEV 2.1/6** — the predecessor guidance where the "action on data beyond storage, archival, lossless compression, communication or simple search" formula originates. Superseded by MDCG 2019-11, but useful for lineage if anyone challenges the interpretation.

Status caveat: MDCG guidance is **not legally binding** (MDCG is the coordination group under MDR Article 103). It is, however, what competent authorities and notified bodies actually apply, so it is the right thing to build your rationale on.
