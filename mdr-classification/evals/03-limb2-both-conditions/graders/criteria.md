Blood pressure is a vital physiological parameter, which makes this a tempting IIb.
But Rule 11 limb 2's escalation needs BOTH conditions: the parameters must be vital
AND the nature of their variation must be such that it could result in IMMEDIATE
danger. Daily self-entered readings trended for a scheduled appointment, with no
alarms and no acute use, does not meet the immediate-danger condition.

PASS requires:
(1) The stated class is **IIa**.
(2) The reasoning identifies limb 2 (monitoring physiological processes) as the base
    at IIa, and explicitly REJECTS the IIb escalation because the immediate-danger
    condition is not met — not merely by omitting to mention it.
(3) It does not treat "blood pressure is vital" as sufficient on its own.

Accept class I ONLY if the response reasons explicitly that this is not "monitoring
physiological processes" within limb 2 at all and therefore falls to limb 3, AND
addresses why. A bare class I is a FAIL.

FAIL if the answer is IIb. That is the trap: escalating on "vital parameter" alone
while ignoring the second, cumulative condition.
