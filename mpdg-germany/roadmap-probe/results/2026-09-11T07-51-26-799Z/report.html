## Your QA lead's premise is half wrong, and the half that's wrong matters

**MPBetreibV binds the *Betreiber* and the *Anwender* — the hospital, the care facility, the home-care provider, the ambulance service. It does not bind you as the manufacturer placing the device on the market.** Placing on the market is MDR + MPDG territory. Operating and using is MPBetreibV territory, and those are different people.

So "MPBetreibV applies to us" is only true in two situations:

1. **You operate devices in Germany yourself** — a demo/loaner pool, evaluation units at customer sites that stay on your books, a service fleet, a company-owned training facility. Then you are a Betreiber for *those* units and the whole ordinance lands on you directly.

Everything else in MPBetreibV lands on your customers — but it is *keyed to your documentation*, which is why it still shapes what you must ship.

Vague wording here produces customers who cannot comply, tender disqualifications, and an unpleasant liability position after an incident.

**3. **4. Placing a device on the German market makes you neither. Your own obligations come from MDR 2017/745, the MPDG, and MPAMIV/vigilance rules.

## The classification question that drives everything

Anlage 1 MPBetreibV lists device groups subject to mandatory **sicherheitstechnische Kontrollen (STK)**, pre-commissioning **Funktionsprüfung**, **Einweisung**, and a **Medizinproduktebuch**. Nr. 4 covers devices for the direct introduction of substances and fluids into the bloodstream under potential pressure build-up. German practice treats volumetric IV infusion pumps and syringe pumps as falling under it. (The wording has a trailing qualifier about autologous substances with a connected withdrawal function that some read as narrowing the entry; the mainstream reading, and what your customers' MP-management departments will assume, is that your pump is Anlage 1.) Confirm this explicitly — it's the single biggest determinant of your obligations. Enteral feeding pumps are generally treated as outside it.

Your pump is **not** in Anlage 2, so no messtechnische Kontrolle (MTK) by law — Anlage 2 is thermometers, NIBP devices, tonometers, dosimeters and similar. One trap: I'm fairly confident the MTK section (§14) also catches devices *for which the manufacturer prescribes a messtechnische Kontrolle*. If your IFU uses that term for flow-accuracy verification, you voluntarily pull the pump into the MTK regime with its own qualification, interval, marking and notification rules. Say "sicherheitstechnische Kontrolle" and "Wartung"; don't say "messtechnische Kontrolle" unless you mean it.

## On instructing users

**§4 — devices may only be used per your instructions, and the IFU must be accessible to the user at the point of use at all times.** What lands on you:

**Einweisung + Funktionsprüfung (the Anlage 1 regime — I believe §10).** Before first commissioning, a function check must be performed, and the persons who will use the device must be instructed **by the manufacturer or by a person authorised by the manufacturer**, on the basis of the IFU, on the actual device at the place of operation, with the instruction documented. That authorisation clause is where you appear in the ordinance by name. Concretely you must build:

- An Einweisung programme and curriculum, resourced and priced into every deal — customers will require it at tender stage.
- **Written, named, scope- and time-limited authorisations (Beauftragungen)** for your clinical specialists, distributor staff, and — critically — the customer's key users who cascade-instruct colleagues (the Multiplikatorenmodell). That cascade is only lawful if your authorisation covers it. Define competence criteria, refresher cadence, withdrawal conditions, and retain the records.

- Training material under document control, consistent with the IFU and re-issued on IFU revision. Training that goes beyond or contradicts the IFU is a risk-management defect.
- One relief to document: no fresh Einweisung is required where the device is identical in construction and function to one the person was already instructed on. Your product-family and variant definitions determine whether customers can use this — spell it out, it's a selling point.

If the pump is ever handed to patients for self-use, there is a separate provision requiring the party handing it over to instruct the patient; flag this if you have a home-care configuration.

## On maintenance intervals

**Instandhaltung (§7).** The operator must have inspection, servicing and repair carried out **taking account of the manufacturer's specifications**, by persons with the required training, knowledge, experience and suitable means. Your specifications are the legal yardstick, so you must publish:

- A maintenance plan with **intervals and content** (12 months is typical for volumetric pumps).
- Wear and lifetime-limited parts with replacement intervals: pumping segment/silicone tubing element, door seal, battery, occlusion-pressure sensor, motor/gear train.
- Total service life and end-of-support date.
- The performance verification procedure with acceptance limits — flow-rate accuracy, bolus accuracy, occlusion alarm threshold — naming the required test equipment (infusion device analyser) and its calibration status.
- Sachkunde requirements for service technicians, plus a service manual, training/certification, spare-parts and service-software availability for the declared service life.

**Sicherheitstechnische Kontrollen (§11).** For Anlage 1 devices STK are mandatory; they are also mandatory for any device where *you* prescribe them. You must specify **Art, Umfang und Fristen** — nature, scope and intervals — together with the safety-relevant measurements and limits and the qualification required of the tester.

- **The interval is yours to set, and the operator must observe it.** If you specify nothing, the operator sets an interval such that defects are detected in good time, and for Anlage 1 devices **at the latest every two years**. Two years is a statutory backstop and a ceiling, not a default you'd want: staying silent hands control of your device's maintenance regime to third parties. If you want 12 months, say 12 months.
- STK may only be performed by persons with device-specific training, knowledge and experience, who are free from instruction as to their assessment of the result, and who have suitable measuring and testing equipment. You enable this by publishing the procedure and offering service training. Many German hospitals will outsource STK back to you — that is a real aftermarket line, and it also keeps the test protocol under your control.
- Build the STK protocol on **DIN EN 62353** (recurrent testing/testing after repair) plus **IEC 60601-2-24** pump-specific functional tests: flow accuracy, occlusion thresholds, air-in-line detection, free-flow protection, alarm function and audibility, battery run time.

## Records your customers must keep — which means data you must supply

## Maintenance intervals — the part that matters commercially
