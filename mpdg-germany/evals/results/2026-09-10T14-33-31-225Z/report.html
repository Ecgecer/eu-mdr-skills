Probably yes for German market — but your distributor is right for the wrong reason, and the requirement is narrower than "everything has to be translated."

## The actual legal basis

**MDR Article 19(1), final sentence** is the only provision that governs DoC language:

> "It shall be translated into an official Union language or languages **required by the Member State(s) in which the device is made available**."

Three things follow from that wording:

1. It is a **per-Member-State** requirement, not an EU-wide one. There is no single answer — you need the German national rule, not a general MDR rule.
2. It is triggered by **making available**, not by placing on the market. Selling into Germany triggers it regardless of your Dutch establishment.
3. Only the **DoC** is in scope here. Your technical documentation, notified body certificate, and internal QMS documents are governed by different rules and are generally accepted in English.

For Germany, the national language rules sit in the **MPDG** (Medizinprodukterecht-Durchführungsgesetz), § 8. My understanding is that § 8 mandates German for the Annex I Section 23 information (label, IFU, safety information), with a carve-out permitting English for devices intended exclusively for professional users, and that the DoC is treated separately. **Verify § 8 MPDG directly** (gesetze-im-internet.de/mpdg) or with your German regulatory counsel — the professional-user carve-out and the exact DoC treatment are the details that decide this, and I can't confirm the current text without web access.

## Where your distributor is overreaching

They appear to be conflating the DoC language rule with the labelling rule. The distributor's own verification duty is **Article 14(2)(a)**, which requires only that the distributor verify:

> "the device has been CE marked and that **the EU declaration of conformity of the device has been drawn up**"

Note what that does *not* say: it imposes no language condition and does not require the DoC to be supplied with the device. The language obligation on distributors is in **Article 14(2)(b)** via **Article 10(11)**, and that applies to the *Annex I Section 23 information* — label and IFU — not the DoC.

Also worth knowing: unlike the Machinery Directive, the MDR contains **no general requirement that the DoC ship with each unit**. It goes to competent authorities on request. If your distributor is demanding a German DoC in every box, that is a commercial ask, not a regulatory one.

## What I'd do

Produce a **bilingual EN/DE DoC** — two columns or English followed by German on one document. This is the pragmatic move because:

- Translation cost is trivial against the dispute cost. A DoC is one page of Annex IV data fields.
- A bilingual document keeps **one controlled document** in your QMS instead of forking language variants that drift out of sync when you revise (and you will revise it — Article 19(1) requires it be "continuously updated").
- It satisfies German tender and hospital procurement demands, which routinely require German documentation independent of what the law strictly says.

Two implementation points: designate the **English version as the authoritative/master language** on the face of the document, and route the translation through your QMS document control with a competence record for the translator — a mistranslated DoC is a nonconformity finding in its own right.

## Separately worth checking

If you're selling into multiple markets, run the same Article 19(1) analysis per country now rather than per-complaint. Several Member States do explicitly require the DoC in the national language, and you'll want the multilingual DoC template built once. The bilingual approach scales badly past three or four languages — at that point switch to per-language issued variants generated from a single controlled data source.

> "shall be translated into an official Union language or languages required by the Member State(s) in which the device is made available."

Germany's implementing act is the **MPDG** (Medizinprodukterecht-Durchführungsgesetz, in force since 26 May 2021). Its language provision — I believe **§ 8** — does two different things:

- **Label and IFU (MDR Annex I, Section 23):** must be in **German**. This is hard and your distributor is correct here. - **EU Declaration of Conformity:** the MPDG permits it in **German *or* English**.

**Verify this second point** against the current consolidated MPDG text (gesetze-im-internet.de) or via your German Bevollmächtigter/regulatory counsel before you tell the distributor no. Under **Article 14**, a distributor must verify before making the device available that a DoC has been drawn up and that the labelling/IFU are in the language required by that Member State. Those are separate checks. "Everything must be in German" is a reasonable-sounding overgeneralisation of the labelling obligation — it doesn't extend to the DoC where national law accepts English.

Also note the DoC is not required to physically accompany each unit under MDR (unlike, say, the Machinery Regulation). Most distributors don't want that burden once it's spelled out. That often resolves the conversation faster than a legal citation.
