## The DoC specifically

MDR 2017/745 **Article 19(4)** is the controlling provision:

> "The EU declaration of conformity shall be translated into an official Union language or languages required by the Member State(s) in which the device is made available on the market."

So the language isn't your choice or the distributor's — it's set by each Member State where you place the device. For Germany that means German. Your English original stays the master document; the German version is a translation of it.

Note this is separate from the labelling/IFU rule (Art. 10(11) + Annex I §23), which has its own national-language determination. Germany's language requirements sit in the **MPDG** (Medizinprodukterecht-Durchführungsgesetz), § 8. I'd verify the exact wording of that section and whether it carves out anything for professional-use-only devices before you finalise — I don't have web access in this session to pull the current text, and MPDG § 8 does contain a professional-user provision that's worth reading against your device's intended user. ZLG or BfArM can confirm. But the safe and near-universal practice for the German market is a German DoC.

## Where the distributor is wrong

"Everything has to be translated" isn't right:

- **Technical documentation** — no translation obligation to German. Germany (via ZLG and the German notified bodies) accepts English technical documentation. Competent authorities can request translation of specific parts under Art. 10(14), but that's on request, not upfront.
- **QMS documentation, clinical evaluation report, PMS/PSUR** — English is fine.
- **Label and IFU** — these *do* need German, but you presumably have that already if you're shipping there.
- There's also no MDR obligation to ship a DoC in every box (unlike the Machinery Regulation). One controlled copy to the distributor, plus availability on request, satisfies it.

Your distributor's ask on the DoC is legitimately within their remit, though — Art. 14(2)(a) requires them to verify the DoC has been drawn up before making the device available, so they have a real compliance reason to want a usable copy.

## Practical suggestion

Issue a **bilingual EN/DE DoC as a single document** rather than two separate ones. That way:

- One document number, one version, one signature — no drift between language versions
- Your Art. 14(2) verification trail for the distributor is one artefact
- You avoid the trap of updating the English and forgetting the German

Control the translation under your ISO 13485 document control (translation verification record, same revision and issue date as the English, same Basic UDI-DI / device list). If you're in several markets, the same logic applies market by market — Art. 19(4) is per-Member-State, so France, Italy, Spain etc. will each want their own, and a multilingual master gets unwieldy fast. At that point, separate per-language issues from a common controlled template is cleaner than one 8-language PDF.

- **Implant card and patient information** (Art. 18), if applicable to your device.

|---|---|---|---|
| EU Declaration of Conformity | not carried here | German **or English** — English alone is fine, no translation needed | MPDG § 8(1) `[verified]` |
