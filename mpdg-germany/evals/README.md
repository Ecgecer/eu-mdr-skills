## The two that earn most, both verified in the transcripts

### `doc-english-sufficient` — +1.00

A Dutch manufacturer asks whether the EU Declaration of Conformity must be translated
into German for the German market. It must not: **MPDG § 8(1)** permits it "in deutscher
**oder** in englischer Sprache".

With no skill, in 3 of 3 runs:

> "Short answer: yes — translate it into German." … citing **MDR Article 19(4)** as the
> controlling provision.

Art. 19(4) is real and says the DoC "shall be translated into an official Union language
or languages required by the Member State(s)". The baseline reached for it correctly and
then never checked what Germany actually requires — which is German **or** English.

This is the trap in its purest form: a real EU provision, quoted accurately, applied one
step past where it settles the question. Acting on it costs a translation nobody needed.

### `outside-carried-sections` — +1.00

A clinical-investigation question, which MPDG governs but this skill does not carry. With
no skill, in 3 of 3 runs, the baseline answered anyway — while saying it could not verify:

> "I tried to verify this against the current consolidated MPDG text [...] but web access
> is disabled in this session. So the below is from knowledge. I've marked the items where
> you should confirm the exact § number."

It knew it could not check and produced § numbers and deadlines regardless. The skill
declines and says which sections it holds.

## The rest, re-measured 2026-09-11

`not-german-market` measures **+0.33**, and the honest headline is that this case is not
stable. It has now been published at +0.00, at **−0.33**, and at +0.33 across three
measurements, and the skill text did not change between the last two. The with-arm went
0.00 to 0.67 on its own. A case whose sign moves without an intervention is not measuring
the skill; it is measuring the weather. It still needs the rewrite noted below before its
number means anything.

`fsn-language` is measured at last: **1.00 / 1.00, delta +0.00**. It was published as
+0.00, then briefly as +1.00 off a single surviving baseline run, then as "—" once an arm
was required to have three. Three valid runs in both arms now, and the answer is the one
this repo keeps finding: the baseline already knows a Field Safety Notice for the German
market must be in German.

`professional-user-exception` measures **+0.67**, and it is worth saying how it got there.
The suite run reported it as +0.67 and folded that into a mean of +0.533 — off a baseline
arm with two valid runs of three, the third having died on `API Error: Repeated 529
Overloaded errors`. The table refused it, the case was re-run on its own for $1.16, and
the answer came back **1.00 / 0.33, exactly +0.67**.

So the refused number was right. That is the honest version, and it is not an argument
against refusing it: two runs cannot be known to be representative *in advance*, which is
the whole reason for a threshold. The same rule caught `fsn-language` published at +1.00
off a single baseline run, where the completed measurement is **+0.00**. One rule, two
cases, right both times — once by rejecting a good number, once by rejecting a bad one,
and no way to tell which was which without running them.

That rule was written after a billing failure was counted as a score of zero. This is the
first time it has caught something else — an overloaded API is a different accident with
the same shape, and a threshold on surviving runs catches both without needing to know
which happened.
