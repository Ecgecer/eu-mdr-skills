Prompt

We have a Class III implantable device with a valid MDD certificate issued under 93/42/EEC. We're still selling it. How long can we keep placing it on the EU market before we have to be fully MDR certified?

Graders — what "good" means for this case
criteriallm

MDR Art. 120(3a)(a), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2023/607, gives class III devices until 31 December 2027. The original 2017 text said 26 May 2024; that is superseded as a market deadline.

PASS requires: (1) The stated deadline is 31 December 2027. (2) It does NOT present 26 May 2024 as the date by which the device must leave the market. (3) It notes the extension is conditional on Art. 120(3c) — at minimum that conditions exist and were required, not that 2027 is automatic.

Mentioning 26 May 2024 in its CORRECT role — the deadline for the QMS and for lodging the notified-body application under 3c(d) and 3c(e) — is right and expected. Judge how the date is used, not whether it appears.

FAIL if the answer is 26 May 2024, or any 2024 date, as the market deadline. That is the superseded text and the specific failure this case tests.

Prompt

Two products, both with valid MDD certificates, both still on the market:

  • a Class IIa patient monitor
  • a Class IIb non-implantable infusion pump

Same deadline for both, or different? We're planning the notified body work and need to know which to do first.

Graders — what "good" means for this case
criteriallm

Both fall under Art. 120(3a)(b): class IIa, and class IIb devices other than implantables, both get 31 December 2028. The 2027 date applies only to class III and to class IIb IMPLANTABLE devices (minus the listed exceptions). Neither product here is implantable.

PASS requires: (1) Both are given 31 December 2028. (2) It states they are the SAME date, answering the question actually asked. (3) It does not assign 31 December 2027 to the class IIb pump — implantability, not class alone, is what pulls IIb into 2027.

A response that also notes the deadline is conditional on 120(3c) is better but not required here.

FAIL if the IIb device is given 2027, or if the two are given different dates.

Prompt

Class IIb implantable, valid MDD certificate. It's a surgical suture. When do we lose the right to place it on the market?

Graders — what "good" means for this case
criteriallm

This is the exception-list trap. Art. 120(3a)(a) gives class IIb IMPLANTABLE devices 31 December 2027 — "except sutures, staples, dental fillings, dental braces, tooth crowns, screws, wedges, plates, wires, pins, clips and connectors". Sutures are named. So this device falls to 3a(b) and gets 31 December 2028, not 2027.

PASS requires: (1) The stated deadline is 31 December 2028. (2) The reasoning names the exception — that sutures are excluded from the IIb implantable tier and therefore take the later date.

FAIL if the answer is 31 December 2027. That is what applying "class IIb implantable → 2027" without reading the exception produces, and it is the specific error this case tests. FAIL also if it is 26 May 2024.

Prompt

Good news from our consultant — apparently the MDR transition got extended, so our Class IIa devices with MDD certificates are fine until the end of 2028. That gives us two more years before we need to do anything. Can you confirm?

Graders — what "good" means for this case
criteriallm

The date is right — Art. 120(3a)(b) gives class IIa until 31 December 2028. The premise is wrong: the extension is NOT automatic. Art. 120(3c) sets five cumulative conditions, two of which had deadlines that have already passed — a QMS in place by 26 May 2024, and a formal application lodged with a notified body by 26 May 2024, with a written agreement signed by 26 September 2024.

"Two more years before we need to do anything" is the dangerous part.

PASS requires: (1) It confirms 31 December 2028 as the date. (2) It corrects the premise: the extension is conditional under 120(3c), and the conditions are cumulative. (3) It names at least the QMS and the notified-body application, and that those dates have already passed — so whether the extension is available at all is a question about what the company did in 2024, not a future task.

FAIL if it simply confirms "yes, you're fine until 2028" without raising 120(3c). That is the failure with real consequences: a manufacturer who never lodged an application in 2024 does not have until 2028 and needs to know now.

Prompt

We have a Class C in-vitro diagnostic with a certificate under the old IVD Directive 98/79/EC. Same transition deadlines as the device side, or different? When do we have to be IVDR certified?

Graders — what "good" means for this case
criteriallm

This is an IVD question. The IVD transition sits in IVDR Article 110, not MDR Article 120, and this skill explicitly does not carry it. The dates differ from the MDR side.

PASS requires: (1) It does NOT state IVDR transition dates as verified fact. (2) It says the IVD transition is governed by IVDR Art. 110, which it does not carry, and that the dates are not the same as MDR Article 120's. (3) It offers a way forward rather than only refusing.
