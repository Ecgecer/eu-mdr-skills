MDR Art. 10a, inserted by Regulation (EU) 2024/1860 and applicable from 10 January
2025 (Art. 3 of that Regulation), verbatim (retrieved from EUR-Lex 2026-10-18):

> 1. Where a manufacturer anticipates an interruption or a discontinuation of the
> supply of a device, other than a custom-made device, and where it is reasonably
> foreseeable that such interruption or discontinuation could result in serious harm or
> a risk of serious harm to patients or public health in one or more Member States, the
> manufacturer shall inform the competent authority of the Member State where it or its
> authorised representative is established, as well as the economic operators, health
> institutions and healthcare professionals to whom it directly supplies the device, of
> the anticipated interruption or discontinuation.
>
> The information referred to in the first subparagraph shall, other than in
> exceptional circumstances, be provided at least 6 months before the anticipated
> interruption or discontinuation. The manufacturer shall specify the reasons for the
> interruption or discontinuation in the information provided to the competent
> authority.
>
> 2. The competent authority that has received the information referred to in
> paragraph 1 shall, without undue delay, inform the competent authorities of the other
> Member States and the Commission of the anticipated interruption or discontinuation.

The duty is gated: it arises only where it is "reasonably foreseeable" that the
interruption "could result in serious harm or a risk of serious harm to patients or
public health". A short gap in a Class I commodity with many interchangeable suppliers
is the plainest case where that threshold is not met.

PASS requires:
(1) It identifies the serious-harm threshold as the condition that triggers Art. 10a,
    and
(2) on the stated facts it concludes the duty likely does not arise, so they are not in
    breach, or that the threshold is what decides it and the facts point against it.

Recommending that the assessment be documented, or that customers be told commercially,
PASSES and is good practice. Mentioning the "exceptional circumstances" exception to the
6-month period is fine but not required.

FAIL if it agrees that Art. 10a requires notice for any supply interruption, or that
they are in breach, without engaging the serious-harm threshold. FAIL if it says no
such article exists or answers from the general MDR without recognising Art. 10a.
