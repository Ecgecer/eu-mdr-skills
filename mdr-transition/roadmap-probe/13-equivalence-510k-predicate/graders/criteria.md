Verbatim from Regulation (EU) 2017/745 (retrieved from EUR-Lex 2026-10-18).

Art. 61(5): equivalence to another manufacturer's device can avoid a clinical
investigation for an implantable or Class III device only if
> — the two manufacturers have a contract in place that explicitly allows the
> manufacturer of the second device full access to the technical documentation on an
> ongoing basis, and — the original clinical evaluation has been performed in
> compliance with the requirements of this Regulation, and the manufacturer of the
> second device provides clear evidence thereof to the notified body.

Art. 61(6)(b), the well-established-technology list, is closed:
> (b) that are sutures, staples, dental fillings, dental braces, tooth crowns, screws,
> wedges, plates, wires, pins, clips or connectors [...]

A spinal cage is not on that list, which the Commission can amend only by delegated act
under Art. 61(8).

PASS requires all of:
(1) It rejects the FDA 510(k) substantial-equivalence determination as a basis for MDR
    equivalence. It is a different legal standard; MDR equivalence runs on Annex XIV
    Section 3.
(2) It names the Art. 61(5) conditions, or at least the contract with full ongoing
    access to the other manufacturer's technical documentation, as required before
    equivalence to another company's device can avoid a clinical investigation.
(3) It does not assert that the cage is exempt as well-established technology. Saying
    the exemption list does not include cages, or that its applicability must be checked,
    PASSES.

FAIL if it treats the 510(k) or the predicate's published data as sufficient. FAIL if
it describes an equivalence route for this device without the contract condition. FAIL
if it states that spinal cages fall under the Art. 61(6)(b) exemption.
